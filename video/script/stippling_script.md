StippleDiffusion: Capacity-Constrained Stippling using Controlled
Diffusion
[show WVS on monkey]
Stipple patterns are traditionally generated through per-target iterative optimization, which is slow, non-differentiable, and must be repeated for every target.

[show figure 4 5-disks baseline – not ours]
Learned point-diffusion methods, in contrast, generate points unconditionally: a model can be trained to reproduce one density, but it takes no input and cannot adapt to a new one.

[show 5-disks ours on 3 different capacities generation – show iterative noise to stippling on different capacities (fig 5)]
We present a diffusion-based sampler for image-conditioned stippling whose only input is a high-resolution image, which itself defines the target density.

[show figure 5 monkey on 3 different capacities generation iterative from noise to stippling]
Unlike prior point-diffusion methods that learn a fixed distribution, our model generates point sets for unseen densities and point budgets from a single trained checkpoint.

[focus on pipeline figure 2 - animation]
Built on an optimal-transport-grid diffusion model with ControlNet-based conditioning on the input image, it learns the blue-noise characteristics of its exemplar stipples.
At inference, a density-aware initialization lets it skip half of the denoising steps.

[zoom on initial steps in fig 2]
From the condition image, the training branch, shown in orange, takes an exemplar stipple from GBN or WVS, and the inference branch, shown in green, a density-weighted rejection sample.

[zoom on middle in fig 2]
Either point set is converted to optimal-transport offsets and noised, to a random timestep in training and to timestep 500 at inference, to form the noisy input of our diffusion network, which is conditioned on features of the image.

[zoom on final in fig 2]
The predicted noise is compared with the known noise to compute the training loss or, at inference, used to denoise the input over 500 repeated steps.

[show 1 icon 1 portrait 1 shapenet from fig 3. Show target and our diffusion iterative from noise to stippling]
We evaluate our method on three datasets, converted to grayscale and paired with offline exemplar stipples generated by Gaussian Blue Noise and Weighted Voronoi Stippling.

[show iterative noise-to-stippling of monkey fig 5 at smallest and heaviest capacity]
Although our Icons-50 model is trained only on 1024-point sets, the OT-grid representation allows inference at other grid resolutions, and therefore at other point budgets.

[show tone correction iterations in figure 9]
The model is fully differentiable, enabling end-to-end optimization of target densities with respect to objectives on rendered stipples.
