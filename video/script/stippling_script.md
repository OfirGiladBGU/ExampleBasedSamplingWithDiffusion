StippleDiffusion: Capacity-Constrained Stippling using Controlled
Diffusion
[show WVS on monkey]
Stipple patterns are traditionally generated through per-target iterative optimization, which is slow, non-differentiable, and must be repeated for every target.

[show figure 4 5-disks baseline – not ours]
Learned point-diffusion methods, in contrast, generate points unconditionally: a model can be trained to reproduce one density, but it takes no input and cannot adapt to a new one.

[show 5-disks ours on 3 different capacities generation – show iterative noise to stippling on different capacities (fig 5)]
We present a conditional point-diffusion sampler that takes a target image and generates a stipple pattern in the style of the sampler that produced its training exemplars.

[show figure 5 monkey on 3 different capacities generation iterative from noise to stippling]
Unlike prior point-diffusion methods that learn a fixed distribution, one checkpoint per dataset and exemplar sampler generates stipples for unseen images and point budgets, without per-target optimization.

[focus on pipeline figure 2 - animation]
Built on an optimal-transport-grid diffusion model with a jointly trained conditioning branch that reads per-point image features, it learns the blue-noise characteristics of its exemplar stipples.
At inference, a density-aware initialization lets it skip half of the denoising steps.

[zoom on initial steps in fig 2]
From the condition image, the training branch, shown in orange, takes an exemplar stipple from GBN or WVS,
and the inference branch, shown in green, a density-weighted rejection sample.

[zoom on middle in fig 2]
Either point set is converted to optimal-transport offsets and noised, to a random timestep in training
and to timestep 500 at inference, to form the noisy input of our diffusion network, which is conditioned on features of the image.

[zoom on final in fig 2]
The predicted noise is compared with the known noise to compute the training loss
or, at inference, used for 500 denoising steps to generate the final stipple.

We evaluate our method on four datasets, converted to grayscale and paired with offline exemplar stipples generated by Gaussian Blue Noise and Weighted Voronoi Stippling.

[show iterative noise-to-stippling of monkey fig 5 at smallest and heaviest capacity]
Although our Icons-50 model is trained only on 1024-point sets, the OT-grid representation allows inference at other grid resolutions, and therefore at other point budgets.

Our sampler is differentiable, which lets us optimize the requested density to correct dot gain in rendered stipples.
